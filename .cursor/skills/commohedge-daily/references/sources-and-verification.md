## 1. Source tiers
- **Tier 1 (preferred):** exchanges and official bodies — ICE, CME/CBOT/COMEX, LME, Bursa Malaysia, GIE AGSI+, ENTSOG, EIA, IEA, OPEC, USDA, central banks, government statements, company filings and press releases (for example Rio Tinto on an incident).
- **Tier 2:** wire agencies and major financial press — Reuters, Bloomberg, AP, AFP, WSJ, FT, CNBC — and specialist press such as Kitco, mining.com, Business Recorder (which republishes Reuters market reports), Brownfield Ag News for US grain and livestock closes, Argus, S&P Global Platts.
- **Tier 3 (cross-check only, label "indicative"):** data aggregators such as Trading Economics and Investing.com. Useful for daily historical settlements and for gas/CFD quotes, but they can lag or show a different contract.

## 2. Never use
Social media, forums, blogs, newsletters of unknown origin, AI-generated or SEO "price today" sites, crypto or promotional sites, and any page with a live ticker widget or without a visible date (widgets are often stale and show another day's prices). Do not rely on a page whose snippet says it is many days or years old.

## 3. How to record a figure
Value | unit and contract | change | type (settlement / intraday / spot / futures) | snapshot time with timezone | source | confidence | conflicts. Prices older than about 24 hours are stale for a daily post: drop them or say so.

## 4. Handling conflicts
Two sources often differ because of contract month, timestamp, spot vs futures, or settlement vs intraday. Record both, explain the likely reason, and use the exchange or wire figure. In posts, give a rounded figure with its snapshot label ("Monday morning"). Never average two quotes.

## 5. Claims that need extra checks
- "Highest since...", "record", "first time since...": confirm against price history, and note whether it refers to settlement or intraday.
- Official rules and targets (EU gas storage target, tariffs, quotas): confirm in an official or Tier 1/2 source. State the rule only if confirmed.
- Armed-group or government claims (strikes, attacks): write "claims" or "reports" unless two Tier 1/2 sources confirm.
- Derived numbers (percent change from a reported change, month-to-date, weekly change): show the calculation and mark it "own calculation" in the footer or the checklist.
- Expiries and rolls: the Brent front-month contract changes on expiry, so levels quoted before and after can differ by several dollars. WTI also rolls. Say which contract you quote.

## 6. Lessons learned (real errors from past sessions)
- EU gas storage target: it was relaxed from 90% to 80% (flexible to 75%). A post that said "90% target" was wrong. Check regulations before citing them.
- Record windows differ by source: one source dated storage records from 2009, another from 2011. Use the more authoritative one (CNBC/Reuters/GIE) or avoid the date.
- Gold has several "prices": COMEX futures close, spot at a fixed time, LBMA fix. Do not draw futures and spot on the same line.
- Morning moves change by the close: a -3% oil move at 07:00 GMT became -7.6% at the settlement. Re-check before publishing late in the day.
- A "weekly change" depends on the snapshot. State it or use the final settlement.
- Single-source headlines (for example an unverified strike report) must not become facts in a post.

## 7. Pre-publish checklist (output as "Points à vérifier avant de publier")
- Every intraday or morning figure, with the time.
- Every figure you derived.
- Every single-sourced or conflicting figure.
- Every unconfirmed claim and how it is worded.
- Any rule, target or "since" claim and where it was verified.
- Reminder: if publishing later in the day, refresh oil, gas and gold.
